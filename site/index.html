Source: Synthetic sample data generated for this repository. These figures are made up. They were generated by a script, not measured, and no city in this table was studied — do not cite them. What is real is the shape of the comparison: car traffic is measured before and after lane construction, and crashes are shown as a rate per 10,000 daily car trips rather than as a raw count, because fewer cars on the road means fewer crashes even when nothing has actually gotten safer. A before-and-after comparison like this one cannot separate the effect of bike lanes from anything else that changed over the same period.
